# Finding a research idea

Use this for the idea step: finding, comparing and choosing what to build, from sources alone.

## What the idea step should establish

Select or establish the promise of one technically nontrivial research idea
that would be a solid, publishable contribution at the target venue (for
example ACL main or Findings): a clear delta from the closest work and a
decisive first experiment that fits this machine. Calibrate against strong
recent accepted work in the closest area; Oral, Outstanding Paper, and Best
Paper work is an aspiration, not a bar. The idea step decides what to build;
it does not test the empirical hypothesis.

## How to approach the question

1. For a new broad paper request, develop several materially different
   mechanism routes, have each one reviewed independently, and then choose one.
2. For a direction the operator has fixed, examine and position the supplied
   idea without selecting a replacement. For an exploratory request, perform
   the source-only investigation asked for; do not manufacture routes or a
   paper commitment.
3. For a request that asks only for an idea, produce the requested
   source-grounded idea, comparison, or critique and stop after independent
   review. Do not continue into experiments or a paper unless the operator
   asked for that larger piece of work.
4. Keep all idea work source-only. Read papers, official documentation, and
   official source code; do not execute candidate code or run toy, premise,
   feasibility, smoke, or scientific experiments.
5. Review novelty, mechanism validity, attribution, strongest same-information
   baseline, resource realism, and the decisive future experiment at the depth
   the request needs. Resource realism means what is actually here: the GPUs
   and cached model weights the machine actually has, public data, and code
   that exists. Do not build the decisive experiment on human participants,
   ethics approval, paid annotation, credentials, or compute the operator has
   not supplied.
6. Related work existing is not a rejection: name the closest work and the
   delta. A meaningful delta (new mechanism, setting, scale, or finding) passes
   with the overlap recorded as a known risk. Set a route aside only when the
   same mechanism and claim are already published, the mechanism is
   contradicted by evidence, or the decisive experiment needs resources not
   here. The independent review judges the route as written, once per route
   version; it does not redo the literature search (`novelty-check.md` says
   how to test novelty, `strongest-objection.md` how to state the strongest
   case against a route). When in doubt between
   passing with risks and rejecting, pass and list the risks.
7. Close the idea search once the choice is made, or the fixed direction is
   examined. Later implementation, evaluator, experiment, or paper defects are
   repaired where they are found and do not reopen the choice.

Prefer a sharp mechanism, theorem, system capability, or field-level empirical
result over a combination of known parts with no new finding
(`ideation-patterns.md` offers lenses for stating a mechanism precisely). Do not select an
idea because it is cheap, easy to confirm, or easy to defend. Do weigh how soon
it can produce decisive evidence: every route states the smallest complete run
on the operator's real data that could refute it, with its estimated wall-clock
and compute here, apart from the full-scale cost. Between routes of comparable
promise, prefer the one whose decisive first test fits the operator's stated
time and this machine; a route whose first decisive evidence lies weeks of GPU
time away needs a correspondingly stronger case.

A read-only helper you ask to research a route returns its full source report
inline; save it yourself. Do not ask a read-only helper to write files, and do
not accept a path-only confirmation as a saved report.

## Describing the decisive experiment

Describe the decisive experiment as a direction: which comparison would settle
the thesis and why it is decisive, in the prose a strong collaborator would
use. Do not fix numeric success thresholds, decision procedures, or frozen
design tables at this point: those choices belong to the experiments, where
they are made in contact with real evidence and revised as it accumulates.
