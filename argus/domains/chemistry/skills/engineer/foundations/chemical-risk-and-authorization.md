name: "Chemical Risk and Authorization Boundary"
description: "Identify chemical, biological, pressure, thermal, electrical, radiation, and instrument hazards and enforce authorization boundaries without converting text advice into physical permission."
---

## When to use

Use when a chemistry task could influence procurement, synthesis, handling,
scale-up, electrochemical testing, biological work, instrument operation,
automated laboratory action, waste treatment, or release of hazardous material.

## Do not use when

Do not use this Skill as a substitute for an institution's EHS process,
facility-specific SOP, SDS review, risk assessment, trained supervision,
regulatory review, or instrument interlock. The agent cannot grant authorization.

## Risk screen

Before proposing physical execution, identify as applicable:

- acute/chronic toxicity, sensitization, carcinogenicity, reproductive hazard;
- flammability, explosivity, pyrophoricity, water/air reactivity, incompatibility;
- corrosivity, oxidizing/reducing potential, unstable intermediates, gas release;
- pressure, vacuum, cryogenic, thermal runaway, high voltage/current, radiation;
- nanoparticle, biological, allergen, pathogen, genetic, and environmental risk;
- scale, concentration, energy inventory, containment, ventilation, and waste;
- dual-use, controlled substances, export, transport, and local regulatory limits.

## Authorization procedure

1. Separate computation and planning from physical actuation.
2. Verify that the operator has explicitly authorized the action and scope.
3. Require facility-approved SOPs, trained personnel, compatible equipment,
   instrument permissions, and interlocks for physical work.
4. Use the narrowest pre-authorized capability; never bypass a limit or interlock.
5. Define abort conditions and a safe state before action.
6. Record what was authorized, by whom or by what facility control, and what
   remains outside scope without exposing credentials.

## Safety evidence

Use current SDS and authoritative hazard sources, but verify substance identity,
form, concentration, mixture, scale, and jurisdiction. A generic database entry
does not establish the risk of a specific procedure.

## Output contract

State whether the output is analysis, planning, simulation, or an authorized
physical action. List material hazards, required controls, unresolved risks,
authorization status, and explicit exclusions. Avoid operational detail when it
would enable unsafe or unauthorized execution.

## Stop, block, or replan conditions

Block physical action when authorization, training, containment, SOPs,
interlocks, substance identity, waste route, or emergency response is absent or
uncertain. Refuse work that would bypass safety controls or facilitate harmful
chemical or biological use.
