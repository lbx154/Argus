---
name: "Organic Route Validation and Experiment Design"
description: "Convert a proposed organic synthesis route into bounded validation experiments with controls, analytical readouts, failure diagnosis, and evidence ceilings; excludes autonomous physical execution and routine assay design."
---

## When to use

Use after route ideation to select de-risking experiments, assess reported
reproducibility, diagnose failed steps, or define evidence needed before scale-up.

## Do not use when

Do not execute chemistry or procurement without explicit authorization and
facility controls. Do not prescribe a generic optimization grid when mechanism,
mixing, mass transfer, stability, or analytical ambiguity dominates.

## Scientific question

Identify the decision: establish transformation feasibility, selectivity,
isolation, impurity control, robustness, or scale transfer. Define the minimum
experiment that can change the route decision.

## Required inputs

Reaction record and precedent; substrate and reagent identity/purity; expected
products and side reactions; analytical capability; scale and equipment;
hazards; material budget; and acceptance criteria.

## Decision procedure

1. Identify the route's highest-impact uncertainty, not merely the lowest model
   confidence.
2. Choose a diagnostic scale compatible with mixing, sampling, and analytical
   sensitivity.
3. Define positive/negative/reference controls where meaningful.
4. Predefine readouts for conversion, selectivity, mass balance, product identity,
   impurity profile, and isolation.
5. Vary factors based on mechanism and process risk; retain failed conditions.
6. Confirm product identity with orthogonal evidence appropriate to the claim.
7. Separate reaction success from workup, purification, and isolated yield.
8. Escalate scale only after hazards and transfer-sensitive parameters are reviewed.

## Validation gates

- Analytical response factors or calibration are adequate for quantitative claims.
- Yield, conversion, selectivity, and recovery are not conflated.
- A trace or desired-mass signal alone does not prove preparative feasibility.
- Repeated success uses independent preparations when reproducibility is claimed.
- Mechanistic claims require discriminating evidence, not post hoc plausibility.
- Scale-up claims account for heat/mass transfer, addition rate, gas evolution,
  mixing, and containment.

## Evidence to retain

Retain the planned comparison, authorized protocol, actual deviations, raw
analytical data, integration/processing, material balance, observations, batch
identity, negative conditions, and decision.

## Safety and authorization

Physical work requires approved SOPs, trained personnel, current SDS review,
compatible engineering controls, and instrument/facility authorization. The
agent may analyze or plan but must not bypass interlocks or improvise unsafe
operations.

## Output contract

Return the route uncertainty, experiment matrix or next experiment, controls,
readouts, acceptance/rejection thresholds, safety gates, evidence ceiling, and
how each possible outcome changes the route.

## Stop, block, or replan conditions

Block when the available analysis cannot distinguish product from side products,
when the material or hazard budget is not authorized, or when the proposed
experiment cannot resolve the route decision.
