## When to use

Use when collecting literature or database evidence, interpreting tool output,
combining heterogeneous sources, writing conclusions, or deciding whether a
calculation or analysis satisfies a research objective.

## Do not use when

Do not mistake a provenance ledger for scientific validation. Domain-specific
controls, calibration, convergence, and independent review still apply.

## Evidence classes

- `retrieved`: copied from a source; accuracy is limited by source quality and
  correct entity matching.
- `curated`: selected or transformed under stated rules; curation can introduce
  exclusions and bias.
- `predicted`: produced by a statistical or machine-learning model; bounded by
  training distribution, split design, calibration, and target definition.
- `computed`: generated by a deterministic numerical method for declared inputs
  and approximations; bounded by model chemistry and numerical convergence.
- `simulated`: generated by a stochastic or dynamical model; bounded by model,
  sampling, initialization, and finite-size/time effects.
- `measured`: produced by an instrument or assay for a specific sample under
  stated conditions; bounded by calibration, controls, uncertainty, and sample
  identity.
- `inferred`: interpretation combining evidence and assumptions; never relabel
  it as a direct observation.

## Claim ceiling

For each material claim, identify the strongest supporting evidence class and
its unresolved limitations. A workflow may lower but never raise its evidence
ceiling without new evidence:

- Literature or database lookup cannot establish a new experimental result.
- Prediction cannot establish synthesis, activity, safety, stability, or
  mechanism without appropriate validation.
- Computation cannot establish physical measurement.
- A measured correlation does not by itself establish mechanism or causality.
- A successful process exit does not establish scientific validity.

## Provenance minimum

Retain source URI or identifier, version or access date, query, selection rules,
original input, transformed input, tool and version, configuration, primary
output, warnings, and the claim that consumes the evidence. Preserve failed and
negative observations when they affected decisions.

## Validation gates

1. Trace each headline claim to primary evidence.
2. Prefer primary literature for methods and novelty; label secondary summaries.
3. Check entity, method, condition, and population alignment.
4. Separate replication from reuse of the same upstream source.
5. Report conflicts rather than silently selecting the convenient source.
6. Mark assumptions and extrapolations adjacent to the conclusion they limit.

## Output contract

For each decision-relevant claim report: claim, evidence class, source or
artifact, validation performed, uncertainty, conflicting evidence, and maximum
defensible wording.

## Stop, block, or replan conditions

Block when a central claim has no inspectable source or primary output, when
provenance cannot distinguish prediction from measurement, or when the requested
claim exceeds the available evidence ceiling.
