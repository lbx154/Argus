## Title
Story And Chapter Planning

## Description
Turn the brief into a plan the draft can execute: a whole-arc `story_plan.json`
and a tight `chapter_goal.json`. Grounds a continuation in the established
`story_state` so the plan never contradicts prior chapters.

## Category
fiction-planning

## When to use
- The `plan` stage of `fiction_writing`, after intake and before drafting.
- To decide what a single chapter/scene must ACCOMPLISH (goal → obstacle →
  irreversible change), not merely what happens.
- For a continuation, to align the next beat with open threads and foreshadowing
  already recorded in `story_state`.

Do NOT use to plan a research paper's sections or a literature survey.

## How to solve
1. **Fix the premise and central question** in one sentence — what the story
   asks and (eventually) turns over. Record in `fiction/story_plan.json`:
   premise, protagonist want vs need, central conflict, arc beats, cast (each
   with a want, a flaw, a secret), and the planned foreshadow→payoff pairs.
2. **Write `fiction/chapter_goal.json`** for THIS unit: the concrete goal, the
   obstacle/pressure, the intended irreversible state-change by the end, the
   viewpoint character, and which open thread(s) it advances. A chapter that
   ends with the world unchanged is a red flag — fix the goal.
3. **For a continuation**, read `story_state` FIRST and cross-check: no
   resurrected dead/absent characters, no contradiction with world_rules or
   timeline, open_threads are progressed not forgotten, planted foreshadowing is
   not leaked early. Note any thread you deliberately defer.
4. **Keep genre/market style as a profile input** (from the brief), shaping
   beats/pacing — do not spin up a genre-specific pipeline.

## When NOT to use
- When there is no brief yet (run intake first).
- To draft prose — planning outputs structure, not narrative text.

## Common pitfalls
- A "scene" with a goal but no change/pressure (people just talk).
- Planning against memory instead of the recorded `story_state` on a continuation.
- Front-loading every reveal; leaving nothing for later payoff.
