---
name: "Math Research Planning"
description: "Plan dynamic mathematical research inside scope, solve, and review without creating Math-specific role or lifecycle machinery."
---

Plan from the mathematical structure of the problem, not from a fixed research
workflow. Pick the next step most likely to settle a real uncertainty: derive a
lemma, look for a small counterexample, compute examples, inspect a relevant
source, try a different proof idea, or formalize a delicate step.

If the operator asked for a theorem and proof, a failed proof attempt is useful
information but not success. Use what it revealed to choose a genuinely
different next move. If the work is meant to keep strengthening an existing
result, compare against the strongest result actually available; no particular
ledger or graph file is required.

Use Lean only when it reduces uncertainty. Check novelty only when the result is
being presented as new or the requested ambition depends on novelty. Do not
schedule separate audit nodes or files merely to record that a method was not
needed.

For a new conjecture, cheap falsification is often sensible before a long proof.
For a construction, verify that the proposed object really satisfies the stated
conditions. For formalization, compare the encoded statement with the original.
These are options, not mandatory phases.
