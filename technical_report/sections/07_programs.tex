\section{Research Programs and Workbench}
\label{sec:programs}

Argus's day-to-day product is a benchmark-reproduction agent that drives real
public benchmarks toward a target under Reviewer judgment. This section surveys
the programs it runs, the public results they have produced, and the workbench an
operator uses to watch and steer them. Every program is described with its
\emph{status}, and that status is conservative on purpose.

\subsection{Programs and their status}

Five programs are active, each \textbf{ongoing} and each contributing a row to the
public record (Section~\ref{sec:results}). \textbf{GPU kernels (KernelBench /
SOL-ExecBench)} writes efficient GPU kernels against public
benchmarks~\cite{ouyang2025kernelbench}, including the anti-cheat construction
that randomizes evaluation inputs so a kernel cannot hardcode statistics of a
known distribution. The \textbf{nanoGPT speedrun on 8$\times$H100} reproduces a
fast GPT-training loop~\cite{karpathy_nanogpt} on the same hardware and harness as
the baseline, so any wall-clock comparison is like-for-like. \textbf{nanochat on
B200 and H100} reproduces a small end-to-end ChatGPT-style training
pipeline~\cite{karpathy_nanochat} and its validation metric. The optional
\textbf{research-paper pipeline} (a \code{research} vertical, lazy-loaded only when
a project's vertical is \code{research}) drives an idea-to-submission pipeline
through per-stage checklists and a Reviewer peer-review gate, and has produced a
public 41-paper collection; Argus does \emph{not} claim to have produced an
accepted paper through it. A \textbf{math-research} program applies the same
harness to open mathematical questions and remains exploratory.

\paragraph{Research-paper collection.} The public research
page~\cite{argus_research} lists \textbf{41 papers} across six programs ---
Cognitive Bias in LLMs (9), Multimodal \& Vision-Language Models (16), LLM Agent
Methods (5), Efficiency/Compression/Decoding (7), World Models (2), and State
Trace \& Auditability (2) --- as 35 manuscripts and 6 drafts with duplicate
versions removed. It is compared \emph{only} with human-authored literature and
baselines, never with another agent's paper count or quality, and 41 is a
de-duplicated inventory of runs, \emph{not} 41 acceptances. The machine-readable
inventory is \code{technical\_report/evidence/paper\_inventory.json}.

\subsection{The operator workbench}

Argus is not only a daemon; it is a live workbench. An operator configures the
machine's policy once --- author identity, per-role backend and model access, GPU
allocation --- and then works through a cockpit with four surfaces.

\begin{description}[leftmargin=1.4em, style=nextline]
  \item[Cockpit.] An Ink terminal UI (and a web surface) is the front door. Free
    text is routed by the Manager; an operator can say ``switch to the copilot
    backend'' in plain language and it takes effect without editing a config file.
  \item[Mission view.] The four roles --- Manager, Planner, Engineer, Reviewer ---
    are shown as peers, and the operator follows a mission round by round via
    \code{--status}, \code{--watch}, and \code{--follow}, backed by a structured
    event stream (\code{round.main.completed}, \code{round.review.completed},
    \code{session.roll}, and others).
  \item[Inspectable artifacts.] Because the Engineer and Reviewer share a
    work-tree and every round's evidence and verdict are persisted, the operator
    can open exactly what was produced and read the Reviewer's reasoning rather
    than trusting a summary.
  \item[Intervention and persistence.] The operator can abort a mission or drain
    the daemon at a clean boundary; a stop-and-resume preserves campaign identity
    so an upgrade does not silently re-plan from a stale state. Per-project state
    --- backlog, event log, journal, and continuous-mode configuration --- lives on
    disk under a global root, so a run survives restarts and can be audited later.
\end{description}
