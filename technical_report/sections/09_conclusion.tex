\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

\systemname is a verification-centered framework for persistent and pivot-capable
long-horizon reasoning. Its premise is that success does not come from rushing in
one direction for as long as possible. The runtime must persist while evidence
supports the current contract, but pivot when verification reveals a failed route,
hidden constraint, invalid evaluator, or incorrectly framed objective. Treating
such evidence as failure to follow the initial prompt is too rigid; allowing an
agent to rewrite its goal freely is unsafe. \systemname instead makes each material
pivot a persistent, evidence-backed, authorized transition.

The framework separates standing intent from the revisable task contract
$K_t=(\iota,o_t,c_t,v_t)$. Manager preserves intent and owns contract and Stage
transitions; Planner converts the reviewed frontier into bounded work; Engineer
produces artifacts and measurements; and a fresh independent Reviewer controls
admission after every normal round. Users enter at consequential decision
boundaries, where evidence and alternatives can refine value-laden goals or
constraints. Verification is therefore not a final checkbox. It determines whether
the runtime should continue, revise the approach, stop, or surface evidence and
alternatives for user clarification or authorization.

Verifier-gated runtime self-evolution remains the execution engine of this framework. Across
Stages, $\Pi_t$ records attempted approaches, measurements, accepted and rejected
routes, Reviewer decisions, skills, and verifier changes. Later missions retrieve
this reviewed state to choose a better approach between the current goal and
constraints while model weights remain fixed. A verified pivot can refine the task
contract; self-evolution prevents the runtime from restarting its search whenever
that contract or Stage changes.

The empirical results remain strong under this framing. Across seven benchmark
arenas, \systemname reaches task-native competitive outcomes; on SWE-Bench Pro it
attains approximately 78\% accuracy versus 59\% for Direct Copilot at
1.41$\times$ aggregate Tokens. Mature operation uses 21\% fewer solve input Tokens
and 15\% less active time than startup, while Reviewer intervention produces 34
verifier recoveries and 22 strict rescue loops. These endpoint results establish
that persistence and verification do not prevent delivery.

The longitudinal cases show why endpoints are not enough. The mathematical
campaign retains one falsified route and six proof-backed frontier updates; a
counterexample, bridge lemma, or stronger bound has value before terminal proof.
The six-paper case survives \PaperCaseRollbacks{} Stage rollbacks, and its
representative campaign turns seven verified no-go routes into a 4,500-row
negative-results study rather than manufacturing a positive claim. These are
examples of intermediate reward and contract refinement: the standing intent is
preserved while the operational target becomes more faithful to evidence.

The same harness does not require step-by-step human supervision once a contract is
actionable. Six paper pipelines span 254 missions and 576 Engineer rounds; the FLA
case proceeds from an operator-supplied target to an autonomously implemented and
validated kernel before external review. Equally important, the runtime can decline
to certify progress: structured blocked, stalled, incomplete, and replan outcomes,
together with 35 first-review blocks, 88 paper-review blocks, and 16 Stage rollbacks,
show honest stopping without preventing later recovery.

Artifact-level outcomes extend the same loop beyond benchmark endpoints. A TileLang
RWKV6 kernel was revised under external human review and merged into Flash Linear
Attention. The supplied ACE-1 record reports synthesizable Sky130 NPU artifacts and
a 75/80 project score; absent raw reproduction inputs, this report treats that
result as operator-reported rather than independently validated.

The current study does not yet prove a causal improvement in user
understanding; that requires prospective collaboration studies. It does establish
the runtime mechanisms, evidence surfaces, and observed trajectories needed to test
that claim. More broadly, the retained objectives, contract revisions, actions,
measurements, verifier decisions, and state transitions define a natural interface
to supervised, preference-based, and reinforcement learning. The long-term goal is
not an agent that merely persists longer on the initial prompt. It is a verified
system that knows when to keep going, when to pivot, and when the meaning of success
must be clarified or authorized by the user.
