\section{Public Results Across Six Arenas}
\label{sec:results}

\begin{designbox}
Report each public result as a scoped claim under the exact protocol it was
measured under, compared human-first, with its evidence tier attached. Units
differ across arenas and are never cross-normalized; a website snapshot is never
presented as a reproduced artifact.
\end{designbox}

Argus publishes a live public record~\cite{argus_results}. This section reproduces
its six current arena results. Figure~\ref{fig:results} presents them as small
multiples --- one panel per arena, each with its own unit --- because the arenas
measure different quantities (kernel rank, bits-per-byte, wall-clock seconds,
solve rate, a gap score) and any shared axis would be misleading.
Table~\ref{tab:results} gives the exact protocol, result, human-first comparison,
and evidence tier for each row.

\subsection{Reading the arenas}

Each number means exactly what its protocol row says and nothing more. On
\textbf{SOL-ExecBench}~\cite{ouyang2025kernelbench} the headline is a \emph{global rank} (\#6) against all
entrants on 101 B200 kernels, with two \#1 finishes and seven top-3 placements;
the two head-to-head wins over Recursive~\cite{recursive2026firststeps} are the
site's note, not the framing. \textbf{nanochat}~\cite{karpathy_nanochat} is reported on both B200 (0.9636
BPB against a 0.9646 human SOTA) and H100 (0.9855 against 0.9879), each under a
5-minute training budget --- lower bits-per-byte is better. The \textbf{nanoGPT
speedrun} reproduces a fast GPT-training loop~\cite{karpathy_nanogpt} on
8$\times$H100 and reports 79.77\,s over $N{=}10$ against the same-device human
record of 80.18\,s. \textbf{AARRI-Bench} reports 63 of 82 research-intern tasks
(76.8\%) against a paper-reported best of 68.3\%. \textbf{Arbor} reports a gap
score of 28.0, above the compared systems (Arbor 20.83, Claude Code 8.33, Codex
6.25). The comparison column is human-authored or paper-reported throughout; no
result is compared to another agent except the already-published SOL-ExecBench
head-to-head.

\subsection{Local corroboration versus website snapshot}

Two of the six rows carry a stronger evidence tier than the others, and the
difference is stated rather than smoothed over. The \textbf{nanoGPT} 79.77\,s row
is locally corroborated by an $N{=}10$ verifier-certified artifact in the
\code{nanogpt-speedrun-h100} project --- the verifier line records
\code{valid=true, n=10}, a validation loss of $3.2776$, a one-sided
$p(\text{mean}<3.28)=0.0040$, a train time of $79.77\pm0.06$\,s, and a
\code{seal=ok}. The \textbf{nanochat B200} 0.9636 row has local frozen-scorer,
single-seed evidence of \code{MEAN\_VAL\_BPB} $=0.963634$ with a frozen-scorer exit
code of 0 and a genuine SM100 flash-attention kernel, in the
\code{nanochat-mission-b200} project. The other four rows --- SOL-ExecBench,
nanochat H100, AARRI-Bench, and Arbor --- are \code{website\_snapshot} with no
in-repository reproduction artifact yet, linked to the machine-readable bundle
\code{technical\_report/evidence/website\_results.json}. No corroboration file was
fabricated where a clean local artifact was not available.

\begin{callout}[What these results do and do not claim]
Each row is a real, scoped claim under its stated protocol and evidence tier. The
table does \emph{not} claim universal state of the art, correctness outside these
protocols, or that a snapshot is a reproduced artifact. Converting the four
snapshot rows into locally corroborated artifacts tied to a named commit is an
explicit roadmap item (Section~\ref{sec:roadmap}).
\end{callout}
