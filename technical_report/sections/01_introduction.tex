\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

A language model is an engine and tokens are its working fluid, but an engine is not
a factory. What made the steam engine consequential was not the discovery that steam
carries energy; it was the century of machinery built to convert that energy into
rotation, and rotation into manufactured goods. Intelligence is at the equivalent
stage. Capability is abundant, and the machinery that converts it reliably into
delivered value is not. \emph{Auto research} is our name for that machinery when the
work requires invention rather than recall---not automated science in the sense of a
machine that discovers unaided, but the pipeline that turns tokens into intelligence
and intelligence into artifacts worth having. Intelligence is not the terminal good;
value is.

An agent harness is the part of that machinery that lets a language model touch the
real world: files, shells, compilers, GPUs, test suites. Building good harnesses is
most of what the last two years of agent engineering produced, and the results are
real. But a harness only executes. Something else has to decide what to attempt,
judge whether the attempt worked, and choose whether the next step is another
attempt, a different route, or a question for a human. We call that role the
\emph{Driver}. In every deployed agent system we know of, the answer to who drives
the harness is that a person does---which also answers how long it can be driven,
since nobody drives anything for eight days without stopping.

This is why agent progress is bounded by a resource that cannot be scaled. The
operator reads the diff, decides it is not what they meant, types the correction,
and the loop advances one step. When the operator goes to dinner, the project stops.
Not because the model is out of capacity, and not because the machine is tired, but
because the only component authorized to say ``that is not right, do this instead''
has gone home. \emph{Human intelligence therefore arrives in discrete bursts},
and every long-horizon agent trajectory is stitched together from the waking hours of
whoever is babysitting it. Measured on the systems available today, that stitching is
dense: a strong coding agent driven turn by turn sustains roughly
\CodexEndurance{} hour before it needs its operator back.

We report what happens when the Driver's seat is handed to the machine. The task the
seat performs is not monolithic, and separating it is the first contribution of this
report. A Driver answers exactly three questions, over and over:

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{Q\arabic*.}]
  \item \textbf{Is this actually done?} Not whether a command exited zero, but
    whether the artifact discharges the obligation that motivated it.
  \item \textbf{What is worth doing next?} Given everything now known, including
    what just failed, where should the next unit of effort go?
  \item \textbf{Does this need a person?} Which of the remaining obstacles are the
    machine's to resolve, and which belong to someone with authority it does not
    have?
\end{enumerate}

It is worth noticing when these questions are hard. Given a dense reward---a test to
turn green, a leaderboard to climb, a latency to halve---all three collapse. Q1 is
answered by reading the number, Q2 by whatever raises it, and Q3 never arises because
nothing is ambiguous enough to escalate. This is why recursive self-improvement has
progressed fastest exactly where such signals exist, and why that progress has not
transferred: \textbf{the Driver's seat is not a separate problem from the missing
score. It is what the missing score leaves behind.}

The tasks that motivate this report supply no such signal. A mathematical campaign
rarely terminates in the theorem it set out to prove, and its intermediate bounds and
counterexamples have no natural numeric value. A kernel rewrite that turns out to be
architecturally wrong yields a large negative number that is nonetheless the most
valuable output of the week. In materials research the measurement that would settle
the question is frequently the thing under construction. Under those conditions an
agent has nothing to hill-climb, and the standard remedy---have a model score its own
progress---reintroduces the problem, since the same class of system produces both the
work and the grade. Section~\ref{sec:no-score} describes what \systemname{} does
instead: rather than manufacture a score, it replaces the scalar with a typed
vocabulary of state transitions in which an informative failure is recorded as
forward motion, and it makes certain readings of the evidence mechanically
impossible---most importantly, that an experiment which never ran can never be
recorded as a refuted idea.

Automating Q2 alone yields an agent that confidently executes a plan nobody checked.
Automating Q1 and Q2 without Q3 yields an agent that spends a credential or force
pushes a branch because no rule told it that this class of decision is not its to
make. The three have to be answered together, by different parties, or the seat
cannot be delegated safely.

\systemname answers them with four model-driven roles over durable state. A
\emph{Reviewer} answers Q1 and is deliberately weak: it runs read-only, so it
\emph{cannot} repair the evidence it is judging even if that would make the round
look better, and it can return \code{blocked}, refusing to certify work rather than
manufacturing a completion. A \emph{Planner} answers Q2 by decomposing the current
research state into bounded tasks, while a \emph{Manager} holds sole authority over
campaign stage transitions so that a single round cannot quietly redefine the
campaign. Q3 is answered by an explicit authority boundary: credentials, payment,
irreversible actions, and publication stop for a human in every autonomy mode, while
an ordinary timeout, failed test, or unavailable backend does not.

This inverts the usual reading of verification. Independent review is normally
presented as a quality filter: run the work, then check it. Here it is structural.
The reason a person must watch a conventional agent is that the component doing the
work is also the component declaring it finished, and no one should accept a
self-certified result on a task that matters. Separate those two, give the certifying
party no ability to edit what it certifies, and let it refuse---and the human can
leave the room. \textbf{Verification is not a quality feature. It is the load-bearing
wall that makes unattended operation possible at all.}

The seat also could not be delegated earlier for a reason specific to research work:
the objective itself moves. A mathematical campaign rarely ends in the theorem it set
out to prove; intermediate bounds, counterexamples, and reformulations are the normal
output. A software request is often underspecified until a candidate implementation
exposes what was missing. In chip design and materials research, the measurement that
would settle the question is frequently the thing under construction. What these
share is \emph{underdefinition}: when work starts, nobody, human or machine, can state
the objective precisely enough to optimize against it. The objective a domain expert
writes at the outset is not a specification handed down to be obeyed; it is their best
hypothesis about what should be built, formed with the least information anyone will
ever have about the problem.

This changes what the Driver must do with it. Prior systems treat departure from the
stated objective as \emph{goal drift}---a failure mode to be detected and suppressed,
on the reasoning that a system allowed to revise its target will degrade it toward
whatever is easiest to finish. That reasoning is sound as far as it goes, but it
assumes the target was right. When it was not, suppressing the departure preserves
the error, and the system spends its budget executing faithfully toward a destination
the evidence has already refuted. \textbf{If the objective is wrong, drifting from it
is the correct behavior.} The genuine difficulty is not that objectives change; it is
that a principled revision and a rationalized failure look identical in the final
artifact.

\systemname{} is therefore \emph{evidence-driven} rather than goal-driven: the
current objective is held as a revisable hypothesis, and what decides whether it
stands is the evidence, not its own authority as the thing that was written down
first. Revision is admitted when it is backed by evidence, crosses an explicit role
boundary, and is recorded with its justification---which is what separates it from
rationalization, since a rationalizing system can produce the narrative but not the
refuting measurement. In practice the runtime carries the framing forward on its own
authority while the evidence supports it, and escalates only when the contradiction
it has surfaced would change something the operator owns---at which point the expert
decides what replaces it and the campaign resumes.
Section~\ref{sec:problem} formalizes this as a working contract
$K_t=(\iota,o_t,c_t,v_t)$, separating standing user intent $\iota$---which is
stable---from the operational objective, constraints, and verification criteria that
evidence may refine, with user-visible decisions tracked in $X_t$.

Prior work has built the harness layer thoroughly. ReAct, SWE-agent, and OpenHands
established practical interfaces between models and external environments
~\citep{yao2023react,yang2024sweagent,wang2024openhands}. The AI Scientist,
CycleResearcher, AutoSci, FARS, and Arbor extend this interaction into iterative
research programs covering planning, experiments, review, memory, and scientific
communication~\citep{lu2024aiscientist,weng2025cycleresearcher,qian2026autosci,
tang2026fars,jin2026arbor}. These systems differ in how they search, remember, and
review, but a person remains in the loop as the party who decides when a result is
acceptable and where to go next. Their reported progress makes the boundary visible:
agent success declines as software-task horizons grow~\citep{kwa2025longtasks}, and
the horizons that matter most are exactly the ones a human cannot sit through.

Three properties must hold before the seat can change hands, and each fails in
long-running agents. \emph{Continuity} fails when a growing transcript is compacted
or dropped, so the evidence that motivated a revision no longer exists when the
revision is proposed. \emph{Acceptance} fails when the component that performs the
work also declares it complete. \emph{Experience} fails when only the final artifact
survives, so refuted routes cannot later be cited as evidence that an objective is
unreachable. Hierarchical and workflow-oriented memory systems address parts of this
problem~\citep{packer2023memgpt,wang2024workflowmemory,xu2025amem}. A larger context
window extends session continuity; taking over the Driver's seat additionally
requires explicit state, ownership, and update rules.

\systemname (Autonomous Research Generation and Understanding System) implements
this as a sequence of \emph{bounded missions} executed against durable project state.
Roles operate across three planes---control, execution, and records---so that
scheduling, work, and record keeping remain separable even though they participate in
one continuous loop. Admitted Stages retain attempted routes, measurements, verdicts,
accepted and rejected results, reusable skills, verifiers, and routing decisions.
Candidate updates enter persistent state only after the authorized role checks the
relevant artifacts and task-native evidence. Model parameters remain fixed, but later
missions therefore begin from a changed search policy rather than merely a longer
transcript. We call this \emph{verification-gated fixed-model runtime
self-evolution}: gated because a candidate becomes reusable only with task-native
evidence and an authorized commit, fixed-model because the weights never move.

\paragraph{Does the seat stay empty?}
The claim is measurable, so we measure it. Across \DutyCampaigns{} campaigns
spanning \DutyWallHours{} wall-clock hours and \DutyEvents{} logged events,
\systemname raised \DutyInterruptions{} requests for a human: one every
\DutyInterval{} hours. Classifying all \DutyInterruptions{} by what they asked for is
more informative than the rate itself. \DutyInfraPercent\% reported broken
infrastructure---a failed GPU driver, corrupted container storage, an authentication
outage. \DutyAuthorityPercent\% requested credentials, budget, or authorization,
which is the boundary behaving as designed. \DutyContextPercent\% reported missing
context files. Only \DutyJudgmentPercent\%---\DutyJudgmentCount{} requests in
\DutyWallHours{} hours---were research judgment, the case where the system had work
available and could not decide how to proceed. With work in hand, duty cycle runs
\DutyCycleLow--\DutyCycleHigh\%. The lowest observed campaign,
\DutyDegradedCycle\%, is the one whose GPU driver and container storage failed
repeatedly: \textbf{the binding constraint on unattended operation is infrastructure
availability, not agent autonomy.}

\paragraph{Do the unattended hours produce anything?}
Duty cycle alone would only prove an expensive idle loop, so we pair it with
delivery. On an SGLang unified-model integration, one engineer working with a
turn-by-turn coding agent invested over \SGLangHumanHours{} hours of human time
without completing the task; \systemname completed the same integration within a
\SGLangArgusHours{}-hour wall-clock envelope and submitted it upstream. On a
FlashAttention-4 adaptation carrying six diffusion-LLM mask families across two GPU
architectures---a direction that had occupied a specialist infrastructure team for
over three months---\systemname landed within \FlashDAGapLow--\FlashDAGapHigh\% of
the native kernel across \FlashDAMissions{} missions and \FlashDACalls{} calls, for
under \FlashDACostCNY{} CNY of model spend, requesting a human
\FlashDAInterrupts{} times in \FlashDASpan{} hours. Kernels authored by
\systemname{} were reviewed by outside maintainers of \code{flash-linear-attention}
and merged with no blocking changes requested---an external check on whether
unattended output arrives as reviewed work or as review debt handed back to a human.

Across seven benchmark arenas the same runtime reaches approximately
\SWEProArgusAccuracy\% on SWE-Bench Pro against \SWEProDirectAccuracy\% for direct
Copilot, 76.8\% on AARRI-Bench, and a 28.0 gap on mathematical data synthesis. It
also declares \ReviewerFirstBlocked{} SWE-Bench Pro tasks blocked rather than
reporting an unsupported success, which is the same gate viewed from the other side:
a system that can leave the room is a system that can also refuse to claim it
finished.

This report makes eleven contributions:

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{C\arabic*.}]
  \item We frame \emph{auto research} as the machinery converting tokens into
    intelligence and intelligence into value, argue that what current systems---ours
    included---actually automate is execution and the human attention bundled with
    invention rather than invention itself, and fix its relationship to
    \emph{recursive self-improvement} as containment: RSI is auto research pointed at
    the production of AI, and inherits every constraint of the general case.
  \item We show how a runtime can iterate and self-improve where no dense reward,
    well-formed objective, or reliable numeric feedback exists---the regime in which
    recursive self-improvement has previously stalled---by replacing the scalar with
    a typed vocabulary of frontier transitions in which informative failure counts as
    progress, separating execution failure from hypothesis refutation so that a run
    which never executed cannot refute an idea, naming the honest non-terminal
    outcomes, and scaling the evidence bar to the maturity of the claim.
  \item We identify the \emph{Driver} as a distinct, previously unautomated role
    sitting above the harness, and decompose it into three separable questions---is
    this done, what is next, does this need a person---each of which must be owned by
    a different party for the seat to be delegated safely.
  \item We show that independent verification is the enabling condition for
    unattended operation rather than a quality feature added after it. A read-only
    Reviewer that cannot edit the evidence it judges, and that may return
    \code{blocked}, is what makes it acceptable for the operator to be absent.
  \item We separate domain expertise from decision authority architecturally. A
    \emph{vertical} declares what counts as evidence in a field---stage order,
    checklists, required artifacts, completion gates---and may raise the evidence
    bar but never lower it; the authority boundary, the review requirement, and the
    escalation path live in a core no domain package can reach, which we verify by
    inspection across all \VerticalTotalLoc{} lines of vertical code.
    \VerticalCount{} verticals ship against a \VerticalCoreLoc{}-line core that does
    not change when a domain is added, the smallest costing \VerticalSmallest{}
    lines, and runtime self-evolution is scoped along the same seam: what proves
    general to a domain is promoted into that domain's contract, not into the core.
  \item We introduce two paired metrics for unattended operation and report them
    from runtime ledgers: \emph{duty cycle}, the fraction of available time in which
    the system is producing, and \emph{interruption rate}, how often a human is
    required. Neither is meaningful alone; together they separate a productive
    unattended system from both an idle loop and a babysat one.
  \item We measure both across \DutyCampaigns{} campaigns, \DutyWallHours{}
    wall-clock hours, and \DutyEvents{} events: \DutyInterruptions{} human requests,
    one every \DutyInterval{} hours, of which only \DutyJudgmentPercent\% are
    research judgment; duty cycle of \DutyCycleLow--\DutyCycleHigh\% with work in
    hand. We further show the residual gap is dominated by infrastructure failure
    rather than agent hesitation.
  \item We argue that long-horizon research requires evidence-driven rather than
    goal-driven control---since an objective written before the work begins can
    itself be wrong, and suppressing departure from it preserves the error---and
    formulate this as evidence-admitted revision over a compact report-level contract
    $K_t=(\iota,o_t,c_t,v_t)$ with explicit user decision state $X_t$. The analytical \code{ManagerAdmit} operator makes evidence, authority, and
    provenance requirements explicit without claiming one atomic implementation API.
    Contract refinement is represented through typed, logged, role-owned surfaces:
    \code{GoalContract} revisions, Manager stage transitions, abandoned approaches,
    and recorded verdict sources give material changes an author, a precondition, and
    an audit trail.
  \item We measure where the runtime spends verification and where it refuses to
    terminate. Of \ReviewerTasks{} SWE-Bench Pro tasks, \ReviewerInvoked{} invoke an
    independent Reviewer and \ReviewerSkipped{} use Engineer self-review. The Reviewer
    withholds completion on \ReviewerRevisionRequested{} tasks, of which
    \ReviewerVerifierRecovered{} later pass the official verifier and
    \ReviewerStrictRescues{} complete the strict review loop; a further
    \ReviewerFirstBlocked{} tasks are declared blocked rather than reported complete.
  \item We report delivery from the unattended hours against human effort on the same
    work. One engineer with a turn-by-turn coding agent spent over
    \SGLangHumanHours{} hours on an SGLang integration without completing it, which
    \systemname finished within a \SGLangArgusHours{}-hour envelope; a
    FlashAttention-4 diffusion-LLM mask adaptation reached
    \FlashDAGapLow--\FlashDAGapHigh\% of native kernel performance for under
    \FlashDACostCNY{} CNY of model spend. We additionally present a six-project paper
    case study spanning \PaperCaseMissions{} missions and \PaperCaseRollbacks{} Stage
    rollbacks, and a mathematical campaign retaining one falsified route alongside six
    accepted theorem-frontier updates.
  \item We separate self-improvement into a parameter-invariant axis, which
    compounds retained state under a fixed backbone and which this report measures,
    and a parameter-changing axis, which moves the capability ceiling itself. We
    report the runtime building and running a from-scratch 1B pretraining stack on an
    eight-GPU B200 node as the entry condition for the second axis, and state
    precisely what remains: training on its own retained trajectories rather than a
    public corpus, and measuring whether a model produced this way improves the
    runtime that built it.
  \item We present frontier GPU and serving infrastructure as the vertical with the
    least forgiving verifier and the most external scrutiny: six diffusion-LLM mask
    families carried into a FlashAttention-4 CuTe DSL kernel within
    \FlashDAGapLow--\FlashDAGapHigh\% of native across two architectures for under
    \FlashDACostCNY{} CNY; a unified-model integration into SGLang validated on real
    hardware; and TileLang and correctness-fix kernels reviewed by outside
    maintainers of \code{flash-linear-attention} and accepted with no blocking
    changes requested. We report this alongside a capability floor across seven
    benchmark arenas showing that the verification machinery does not cost end-task
    performance.
\end{enumerate}
